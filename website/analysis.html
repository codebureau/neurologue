Analysis

Five views that turn your captured thoughts into structured insight.

Neurologue's analysis views sit alongside Library and Themes in the left navigation rail. Each view offers a different lens on your corpus: from a high-level daily dashboard to deep contradiction detection, priority scoring, an interactive knowledge graph, and a time-travel replay of your past thinking.

Dashboard

A single-screen overview of your corpus health and recent activity.

Click Dashboard in the left navigation rail to open a bird's-eye view of your knowledge base. The dashboard updates on demand — click the ↻ refresh button in the top-right to reload all data.

Stat cards

Four cards at the top show headline numbers:

CardWhat it shows
Total notesAll entries in the corpus. Click to jump to the Library.
This weekEntries captured in the last 7 days. Click to jump to the Library.
Open loopsQuestions, Tasks, and Decisions that haven't been revisited in 14+ days. Click to jump to the Priorities view's open loops list.
ConflictsActive (unresolved) contradiction pairs. Click to jump to the Conflicts view.

Thought density

A 14-day sparkbar sits below the stat cards. Each bar represents a single day; its height shows how many entries were captured that day. Use it to spot capture habits — days you wrote a lot versus quiet periods.

Active and emerging themes

The left column shows two lists:

  • Active themes — themes with the most entries captured in the last 7 days. These are the areas currently occupying your mind.
  • Emerging themes — themes with a recent spike in activity relative to their usual pace. Something new is picking up momentum.

Click any theme name to jump directly to that theme in the Themes view.

Open loops and recent captures

The right column shows:

  • Open loops — entries of type Question, Task, or Decision captured more than 14 days ago that haven't been edited since. These represent unresolved threads. Click any entry to open it in the Library.
  • Recent captures — the last few entries you saved, regardless of category or theme.

Portfolio scope toggle

If the Portfolio feature is enabled, a scope toggle appears below the dashboard header. Switch between This profile (current active profile only) and All profiles to see a combined overview across all your portfolios.

Conflicts

Pairs of entries that contradict each other — detected automatically.

Click Conflicts in the left navigation rail. The view has two columns: a filterable list of detected conflict pairs on the left, and a detail panel on the right for reviewing and resolving each pair.

How conflicts are detected

The background worker periodically compares entries that share a theme or tag and sends candidate pairs to the LLM with a prompt asking: "Do these two statements genuinely contradict each other?" Pairs that the LLM judges as contradictory are stored as conflicts.

Detection runs automatically on a schedule (default: every 15 minutes for up to 15 pairs). You can also trigger an immediate unlimited scan with the Scan now button at the bottom of the list. Scan scope and timing are configurable in Settings → Worker.

Requires Ollama. Contradiction detection uses the configured LLM model. If Ollama is not running when a scan runs, no new conflicts are detected. Existing stored conflicts are always visible.

Reviewing a conflict

Click any row in the list to open the conflict in the detail panel. The panel shows:

  • The two conflicting entries, displayed side by side in their own cards
  • The theme and date of each entry
  • The current status badge (Active, Resolved, or Dismissed)

Resolving or dismissing

For each active conflict you have two options:

  • Mark resolved — use this when you've addressed the contradiction in your thinking. Optionally add a resolution note (e.g. "Changed my view after learning X") before clicking.
  • Dismiss — use this when the detection was a false positive, or when the contradiction doesn't matter. Dismissed pairs are hidden from the Active list.

You can view resolved and dismissed pairs using the filter tabs (Active / Resolved / Dismissed) at the top of the list.

Scan settings

Three settings control how conflict scanning works — adjust them in Settings → Worker:

  • Contradiction scan interval — how often the scheduled scan runs (default: 900 s / 15 min)
  • Scheduled scan pair limit — how many pairs the scheduled scan checks per run (default: 15). Manual "Scan now" is always unlimited.
  • Contradiction scan scope — Theme only (pairs within the same theme), Tags also (entries sharing a tag are also compared), or Global (all-pairs — expensive on large corpora).

Priorities

Theme-level priority scoring based on energy, value, obligation, and motivation.

Click Priorities in the left navigation rail. The view ranks your themes using the EVOM model — a four-axis scoring system derived from the language patterns in your entries.

The EVOM model

Each theme is scored on four dimensions, each on a 0–1 scale. The LLM reads the theme's recent entries and assigns scores based on the language used:

DimensionWhat it measures
Energy (E) How much enthusiasm, engagement, or positive momentum is expressed. High energy = you're engaged and motivated right now.
Value (V) How important this area appears to be — based on the weight and frequency of its entries. High value = this matters a lot to you.
Obligation (O) How much pressure, duty, or external commitment is implied. High obligation = you feel you must do this.
Motivation (M) Whether the theme's entries reflect intrinsic (self-driven) or extrinsic (pressure-driven) motivation. High = intrinsic.

The overall priority score is a weighted combination of these four dimensions. Themes are ranked in the list by priority score, highest first.

Drift sparkline

Below the EVOM bars, a small sparkline chart shows how Energy (solid line) and Priority (dashed line) have changed across re-computation runs over time. Use it to spot trends: is this theme gaining energy? Declining?

Neglected obligation alert

If a theme has high Obligation but consistently low Energy, a ⚠ Neglected obligation alert appears in the sparkline panel. This flags a potential source of stress: something you feel you must do, but aren't currently engaging with.

Energy × Value quadrant

A 2×2 quadrant plots each theme at the intersection of its Energy and Value scores. Use it to quickly classify your themes:

QuadrantInterpretation
High E, High V (top-right)Engaged and important — your core priorities right now.
High E, Low V (top-left)Enthusiastic but potentially unfocused — fun but not strategic.
Low E, High V (bottom-right)Important but not energised — possible neglected obligation or blocked progress.
Low E, Low V (bottom-left)Low engagement and low importance — candidates for deprioritising.

Signal stats

Below the quadrant, three signal statistics provide context for the scores:

  • Open loops — Questions, Tasks, and Decisions within this theme that haven't been revisited recently.
  • Entries (30 d) — how many entries were captured for this theme in the last 30 days, giving a sense of recent activity volume.
  • Computed — when the EVOM scores were last calculated.

Recomputing priorities

Priorities are recomputed automatically after each clustering run. Click the ↻ button in the list header to trigger a manual recomputation. This requires Ollama to be running.

Graph

An interactive force-directed map of your knowledge — entries as nodes, similarity as edges.

Click Graph in the left navigation rail to open the Knowledge Graph. The graph renders every embedded entry as a node, and draws edges between entries that exceed a similarity threshold — so the layout naturally groups related entries into visible clusters that correspond to your themes.

Interacting with the graph

  • Drag nodes to rearrange the layout manually.
  • Scroll / pinch to zoom in and out.
  • Click a node to select it and open a brief tooltip showing the entry preview.
  • Drag the canvas (not a node) to pan.

Toolbar controls

  • ↻ Refresh — reloads the graph data from the database. Use this after adding new entries that have been embedded.
  • Reset — resets zoom level and re-runs the force layout from scratch, without fetching fresh data.
  • The node count displayed in the toolbar shows how many entries are currently rendered.

Only entries that have been embedded by the background worker appear in the graph. Newly captured entries may not appear until the worker has processed them.

Memory Replay

Travel back in time through your captured thoughts — by month, by comparison, or by finding ideas you've neglected.

Click Replay in the left navigation rail. The view has three tabs:

Month view

Select a calendar month from the dropdown. Neurologue shows a summary of that month's activity:

  • Top themes — the themes most active during that month, with entry counts.
  • Entries from this month — all entries captured during the selected month, in reverse chronological order.
  • A total entry count badge shows how many entries you captured that month.

Click any entry in the list to jump to it in the Library view.

Compare periods

Choose two date ranges — Period A and Period B — and click Compare. Neurologue performs a theme diff between the two periods and shows results in three columns:

ColumnWhat it shows
Faded themesThemes present in Period A but absent or much quieter in Period B — subjects you were thinking about then but have since moved on from.
In both periodsThemes active in both windows — persistent preoccupations.
New themesThemes present in Period B but not in Period A — new areas of thinking that emerged more recently.

Abandoned ideas

The Abandoned ideas tab surfaces entries of type Idea, Question, or Decision that were captured more than 30 days ago and haven't been edited or revisited since.

These are open threads — things you were thinking about and never returned to. Reviewing them occasionally can surface forgotten insights, spark new connections, or help you decide to formally close them.

Click any entry in the list to open it in the Library view.
